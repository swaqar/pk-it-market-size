02 — The capture-the-outflow thesis (the cloud / AI gap)

The sharpest single number in the model is not a revenue figure, it is a gap: Pakistan's total cloud spend ceiling is roughly $800M-1.2B, but 90%+ of it leaves the country because there is no AWS, Azure or GCP region (Pakistan is served from Mumbai and Dubai). Domestic providers capture only the $180-230M floor.

The two estimates are not contradictory once scope is separated: a bottom-up count of what local providers bill (floor) versus a top-down count of everything Pakistani buyers pay, mostly to offshore regions (ceiling). The commercial opportunity is the difference.

04 — Export trajectory vs the official ambition

  • Actual: $4.6B FY25-26, ~16% 5-yr CAGR, $400M short of the $5B target.
  • Ambition: official $10-15B by 2030.
  • Gap to close: reaching the ambition needs sustained 25-30% annual growth, roughly double the current trajectory. Not on track without an accelerant (higher-value products, GCC/ASEAN expansion, sovereign-cloud push converting domestic demand).

Bottleneck: the developer population is growing ~8x in six years, but only 10-18% of new graduates are industry-deployable. A huge talent stock with a wide employability filter is the structural limit on the $10-15B ambition and on the sovereign-cloud engineering build-out.

06 — Data gaps (kept honest)

  • No official SBP segment split of the $4.6B (software dev vs BPO vs call centre vs gaming). Only freelance is itemised.
  • No published domestic IT-services revenue survey (enterprise software spend in rupees does not exist publicly).
  • No reachable Gartner/IDC primary Pakistan figure (values here are second-hand).
  • Upwork / Fiverr Pakistan share (12-13%) has no primary document.
  • Gaming export revenue is unquantified.
  • SBP and FBR primary sites are bot-blocked; several anchors are press quotes of official data and are labelled UNVERIFIED accordingly.
